Why manganese steel works

It gets harder where it's hit.

01 · As cast and quenched

Soft enough to take the shock.

Austenitic manganese steel leaves our water quench at about 190–240 HB. That's tough enough to absorb heavy blows without cracking.

02 · Every impact

Each blow changes the surface.

Every rock that strikes the liner deforms the working face, and the steel responds by hardening exactly where it is hit.

03 · In service

A hard face over a tough core.

The face work-hardens to above 500 HB while the core underneath stays tough. The part resists gouging without turning brittle.

04 · Where standard grades stop

The grade decides the wear life.

Not every crusher delivers the impact manganese needs, and no two rocks wear the same. That's why we pour 14, 18 and 22% Mn, chromium-molybdenum grades, and our own MNX.

MNX

Our own grade for the zones where conventional manganese reaches its limits.

MNX is a manganese steel grade we developed ourselves for high-wear zones. Where a standard grade wears out early, we look at the crusher, the rock and the failed part, then tell you honestly whether MNX, or another of our grades, will last longer.

  • Custom-developed by our own metallurgists
  • Chemistry checked by spectrometer before every pour
  • Heat treated and water quenched in our own furnaces

Fits the first time.

A liner that doesn't seat costs you a shutdown. Every part we ship is checked against its drawing for the dimensions that decide fit: seats, bores and mating faces. Machined seats are turned on our own vertical lathes.

  • Seats and bores machined on 7 VTLs up to 2,300 mm
  • Chemistry of every heat checked before pouring
